# Save the Cat 15-Beat Structure

## When to Use
- Planning a new script's structure before writing
- Evaluating an existing script's pacing
- Creating a beat sheet for team collaboration
- Diagnosing structural problems in a draft

## The 15 Beats

### Act 1 (Pages 1-25)

1. **Opening Image** (Page 1)
   - A visual that establishes tone, mood, and the protagonist's "before" state
   - Should contrast with the Final Image
   - Example: A lonely person in a crowded room

2. **Theme Stated** (Page 5)
   - Someone (not the protagonist) says the theme out loud
   - The protagonist doesn't understand it yet
   - Example: "You know, family is everything" — said by a character the protagonist dismisses

3. **Set-Up** (Pages 1-10)
   - Introduce protagonist's world, flaws, and missing piece
   - Show what needs fixing (the protagonist's "before" state)
   - Plant seeds for later payoffs

4. **Catalyst** (Page 12)
   - The inciting incident that disrupts the status quo
   - Can be good news or bad news, but it must force change
   - The protagonist's reaction: denial, resistance, or reluctant acceptance

5. **Debate** (Pages 12-25)
   - The protagonist hesitates: "Should I go?"
   - Show the fear, the stakes, the reasons not to act
   - End with the protagonist making a choice

### Act 2A (Pages 25-50)

6. **Break into Two** (Page 25)
   - The protagonist commits to the journey
   - Crossing the threshold — no going back
   - The "before" world is left behind

7. **B Story** (Page 30)
   - A subplot (often a romance or friendship) that carries the theme
   - The B-story character is often the "theme stated" character
   - This is where the protagonist learns the lesson

8. **Fun and Games** (Pages 30-55)
   - The "promise of the premise" — what the audience came to see
   - Explore the new world, play with the concept
   - For a comedy: the funniest scenes. For a thriller: the most thrilling scenes

9. **Midpoint** (Page 55)
   - A false victory or false defeat
   - Stakes are raised
   - The "fun and games" are over — now it gets serious
   - A clock often starts ticking

### Act 2B (Pages 55-75)

10. **Bad Guys Close In** (Pages 55-75)
    - External pressures mount (antagonists, obstacles)
    - Internal pressures mount (team fractures, doubt)
    - Things fall apart despite the protagonist's efforts

11. **All Is Lost** (Page 75)
    - The lowest point — the "whiff of death"
    - A mentor dies, a relationship ends, a plan fails completely
    - The protagonist's "before" state seems better than this

12. **Dark Night of the Soul** (Pages 75-85)
    - The protagonist processes the loss
    - "Why did I even try?" — the moment of giving up
    - Then: the epiphany. The theme is finally understood

13. **Break into Three** (Page 85)
    - The protagonist knows what must be done
    - The A-story and B-story merge
    - A new plan is formed — this time with the lesson learned

### Act 3 (Pages 85-110)

14. **Finale** (Pages 85-110)
    - The protagonist executes the new plan
    - Final confrontation with the antagonist
    - The "after" state is achieved through sacrifice and growth
    - Three parts: gathering the team, executing the plan, the high tower surprise

15. **Final Image** (Page 110)
    - A visual that shows the protagonist's "after" state
    - Should directly contrast with the Opening Image
    - Example: The same lonely person, now surrounded by family

## Genre-Specific Notes

### Short Video (60-90 seconds)
- Compress to 7 beats: Hook → Problem → Catalyst → Try → Fail → Epiphany → Resolution
- Opening Image = first 3 seconds (critical for retention)
- Midpoint = 30-second mark (algorithmic drop-off point)
- Final Image = call to action

### Brand/Marketing Video
- Beat 1: Pain point (target audience's "before")
- Beat 4: Product as catalyst (not protagonist — user is protagonist)
- Beat 8: Demonstration (fun and games = product in action)
- Beat 11: Objection handling (the "all is lost" = doubt)
- Beat 14: Transformation (the "after" state)
- Beat 15: CTA (the final image = desired customer state)
