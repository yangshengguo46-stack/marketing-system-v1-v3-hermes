# Character Bible

## When to Use
- Starting a new narrative project with multiple characters
- Developing a character series (e.g., recurring personas for short videos)
- Auditing character consistency across episodes/scripts
- Onboarding new writers to an existing project

## Relationship Mapping

### Relationship Types
- **Ally**: Supports the protagonist, may have their own agenda
- **Antagonist**: Directly opposes the protagonist's goal
- **Mentor**: Provides guidance, often pays a price
- **Shapeshifter**: Loyalty is ambiguous, keeps audience guessing
- **Trickster**: Provides comic relief, may deliver hard truths
- **Foil**: Highlights protagonist traits through contrast

### Relationship Dynamics
For each pair, document:
1. **Power dynamic** — who has power, and how does it shift?
2. **Information asymmetry** — who knows what the other doesn't?
3. **Emotional charge** — love, hate, resentment, longing, guilt?
4. **History** — what happened before the story started?
5. **Trajectory** — where is this relationship heading?

## Consistency Tracking

### Per-Episode Checklist
- [ ] Character's voice is consistent with their profile
- [ ] Actions align with established motivations
- [ ] No contradictions with previous episodes
- [ ] Character arc is progressing, not resetting
- [ ] Relationships evolve naturally

### Series-Level Audit
- Track each character's state across episodes
- Flag any "character reset" (forgetting growth from previous episodes)
- Verify side characters don't overshadow the protagonist
- Ensure antagonists remain credible threats
