# Dialogue Craft

## When to Use
- Writing dialogue for a scene
- Revising existing dialogue
- Auditing a draft for common dialogue problems
- Developing character voice

## Core Principles

### 1. Subtext Over Text
The audience should always understand more than the characters say.

**Bad (on-the-nose):**
> "I'm so angry with you for lying to me."

**Good (subtext):**
> "Interesting. And when exactly were you going to mention this?"

### 2. Conflict in Every Line
Every exchange should have tension — even between allies.

**Bad (no conflict):**
> "Let's go to the store."
> "Okay, let's go."

**Good (conflict):**
> "We need to go to the store."
> "Now? It's almost midnight."
> "That's kind of the point."

### 3. Action Over Speech
Characters reveal themselves through what they do, not what they say.

**Bad (telling):**
> "I'm a very careful person. I always check everything twice."

**Good (showing):**
> She locks the door. Checks it. Walks three steps. Comes back. Checks it again.

### 4. Interruption and Incompletion
Real people don't speak in complete paragraphs. They interrupt, trail off, and talk past each other.

**Techniques:**
- Interruption: "I thought you said—"
- Deflection: "Anyway, about tomorrow..."
- Non-sequitur: Changing the subject to avoid
- Silence: What's NOT said is often loudest

## Dialogue Audit Checklist

### Per-Line Check
- [ ] Is there subtext? (Does the character mean something other than what they say?)
- [ ] Is there conflict or tension?
- [ ] Does this line sound like THIS character, or could anyone say it?
- [ ] Is exposition being delivered through conflict?
- [ ] Is the line shorter than it needs to be? (Brevity = power)

### Per-Scene Check
- [ ] Does every character have a distinct voice?
- [ ] Is there at least one moment of surprise?
- [ ] Does the scene end on a hook, not a resolution?
- [ ] Are there "talking heads" pages? (Add action beats)
- [ ] Does the longest speech belong to the character with the most power?

### Per-Character Check
- [ ] Consistent vocabulary level across all scenes
- [ ] Catchphrases used sparingly (not every scene)
- [ ] Emotional range matches character profile
- [ ] Lying patterns consistent (what they avoid saying)

## Character Voice Differentiation

### Voice Dimensions

| Dimension | Low | High |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Sentence length | Short, punchy | Long, flowing |
| Vocabulary | Simple, direct | Complex, literary |
| Emotional expression | Stoic, controlled | Volatile, expressive |
| Humor | None / dry | Frequent / broad |
| Directness | Circumlocutory | Blunt |
| Self-awareness | Oblivious | Meta-commentary |

### Quick Voice Test
Take a line of dialogue. Could you identify the speaker without attribution tags?
If not, the voice isn't distinct enough.

## Short Video Dialogue Patterns

### 60-Second Format
- **Opening line** (0-3s): Must hook — question, bold statement, or conflict
- **Mid-section** (3-45s): Rapid exchanges, no monologues > 10 words
- **Punchline** (45-55s): Callback to opening or twist
- **CTA** (55-60s): Direct address to camera, breaks fourth wall

### Platform-Specific Voice

| Platform | Dialogue Style | Pacing | Notes |
|----------|---------------|--------|-------|
| Douyin | Fast, punchy, direct | 2-3 lines per beat | Catchphrase in first 3 seconds |
| Xiaohongshu | Intimate, reflective | Slower, more pauses | Voice-over style, not dialogue |
| Bilibili | Witty, referential | Moderate | Inside jokes, callbacks |
| Weibo | Sharp, topical | Text-first | Dialogue in captions, not spoken |

## Common Problems & Fixes

| Problem | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| On-the-nose | Characters say exactly what they feel | Replace with action or indirect reference |
| Exposition dump | Long speech explaining backstory | Break into argument between characters |
| Everyone sounds the same | Can't tell who's talking | Assign each character a voice dimension profile |
| Talking heads | Page after page of pure dialogue | Insert action beats every 3-4 lines |
| Monologue syndrome | One character talks for a paragraph | Interrupt with other characters' reactions |
| Over-attributed | "He said angrily" | Cut adverbs; let dialogue convey emotion |
