# Shot Design

## When to Use
- Planning a storyboard shot by shot
- Directing a scene's visual language
- Creating a shot list for production
- Enhancing visual storytelling in short videos

## Shot Types

| Shot Type | Framing | Emotional Effect | When to Use |
|-----------|---------|------------------|-------------|
| Extreme Wide (EWS) | Subject tiny in environment | Isolation, scale, context | Establishing, aftermath |
| Wide (WS) | Full body + environment | Setting, relationship to space | Scene openings, action |
| Medium (MS) | Waist up | Neutral, conversational | Dialogue, standard content |
| Medium Close (MCU) | Chest up | Intimate but professional | Talking head, interviews |
| Close-Up (CU) | Head/face | Emotion, intensity | Emotional beats, reactions |
| Extreme Close (ECU) | Eyes/detail | Shock, intimacy, detail | Key moments, objects |
| Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) | Behind one character | Conversation, power dynamic | Dialogue scenes |
| POV | Character's perspective | Immersion, identification | Action, discovery |

## Camera Movement

| Movement | Effect | When to Use |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Static | Stability, control | Formal scenes, interviews |
| Pan | Surveying, following | Revealing environment |
| Tilt | Revelation, scale | Showing height, subject |
| Dolly In | Increasing intimacy | Emotional escalation |
| Dolly Out | Increasing distance | Emotional withdrawal, revelation |
| Tracking | Following action | Movement, energy |
| Crane Up | Liberation, scale | Triumph, endings |
| Handheld | Urgency, realism | Action, documentary feel |
| Steadicam | Smooth following | Continuous shots, elegance |

## Power Dynamics Through Camera

### Dominance
- **Low angle**: Subject appears powerful, imposing
- **High angle**: Subject appears vulnerable, small
- **Dutch tilt**: Unease, disorientation
- **Eye-level**: Neutral, equal footing

### Focus as Power
- **Rack focus**: Shift focus from one subject to another = power transfer
- **Shallow depth of field**: Isolate subject, blur background = intimacy
- **Deep focus**: Everything sharp = context matters, viewer chooses where to look

## Lighting Grammar

### Three-Point Lighting
- **Key light**: Primary source, defines shape (45° from camera, eye level)
- **Fill light**: Softens shadows from key (opposite side, lower intensity)
- **Back light**: Separates subject from background (behind subject, above)

### Lighting Moods

| Setup | Mood | Use Case |
|-------|------|----------|
| High key (low contrast) | Bright, optimistic, clean | Product demos, lifestyle |
| Low key (high contrast) | Dark, mysterious, dramatic | Thriller, luxury, night |
| Rembrandt (triangle on cheek) | Classic, painterly | Portraits, interviews |
| Split lighting (half face) | Inner conflict, duality | Character moments |
| Butterfly (shadow under nose) | Glamorous, authoritative | Beauty, fashion |
| Natural/window light | Authentic, intimate | Vlog, documentary |

### Color Temperature
- **Warm (3200K)**: Cozy, intimate, nostalgic — indoor, golden hour
- **Cool (5600K)**: Clean, professional, modern — office, daylight
- **Mixed**: Tension, unease — horror, drama

## Short Video Shot Patterns

### 60-Second Douyin/TikTok Pattern
```
Shot 1 (0-3s):   MCU, direct to camera, hook line
Shot 2 (3-8s):   CU, reaction or demonstration
Shot 3 (8-15s):  WS, context or B-roll
Shot 4 (15-25s): MCU, back to camera, key information
Shot 5 (25-35s): CU, emotional beat or product close-up
Shot 6 (35-45s): MS, demonstration or proof
Shot 7 (45-52s): MCU, payoff or twist
Shot 8 (52-58s): CU, call to action
Shot 9 (58-60s): Logo/end card
```

### Product Showcase Pattern
```
1. ECU product detail → 2. CU hands using product →
3. MS person + product → 4. WS lifestyle context →
5. CU result/benefit → 6. MCU direct to camera CTA
```

### Story/Emotional Pattern
```
1. WS establishing → 2. MS character → 3. CU emotion →
4. OTS interaction → 5. CU reaction → 6. MS resolution →
7. WS aftermath → 8. MCU to camera, reflection
```

## Shot List Template

```markdown
## Scene [X]: [Scene Title]

| # | Shot | Type | Movement | Lighting | Duration | Description |
|---|------|------|----------|----------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | 1A  | MCU | Static   | Key+Fill | 3s       | Opening hook |
| 2 | 1B  | CU   | Dolly in | Low key  | 2s       | Emotional beat |
```

## Composition Rules

### Rule of Thirds
Place key elements on the intersections of a 3×3 grid. Creates dynamic tension vs. center framing (which feels static/formal).

### Leading Lines
Use natural lines (roads, corridors, arms) to direct the viewer's eye to the subject.

### Depth Layering
Foreground + midground + background = visual depth. Even in 2D video, layering creates immersion.

### Head Room
- Too much: Subject looks lost
- Too little: Feels claustrophobic
- Rule: 1/3 of the frame above the head for MCU/CU

### Look Room
When subject looks left/right, leave space on the side they're looking toward. Creates visual flow.
